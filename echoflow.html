EchoFlo

Your all feature music creator

Bring your remix to life
with music

Case study · Gen-AI · Lead designer

Making a powerful AI feel guided, not guessed.

EchoFlow turns a video, an image, an audio clip or a line of text into original music. I led the design from zero to one. The hardest part wasn’t the model. It was helping people know what to ask it for.

Role
Lead designer
Scope
0–1 product, end to end
Timeline
2025/08 – 2025/11
Platform
Responsive web & mobile app

Challenge

Creating music masterpieces with AI

Every attempt costs the user. A video or audio generation starts at 50 credits, and people weren’t sure what the model could do or what they could upload. Without guidance, most attempts would be guesses, and guesses were expensive.

How do we make every generation count?

  • Multimodal Design

    Experience for video, audio, and images

  • Trust & Transparency

    Let the user know what’s going on

  • Zero-to-One Vision

    Clear goal on each stage

  • Craft & Delight

    Intuitive UI, color, and rhythm

Key decisions

Four calls that shaped the product

01

Flow design

Four media types, four different flows

The default
One unified workflow for everything. It was the simplest thing to build.
Why I pushed back
A video, a song, a set of photos and a sentence are very different starting points. One shared form means asking questions that don’t apply and hiding the ones that do.
What I did
Each media type gets its own steps and its own guidance, all inside one visual system so it still feels like a single product.
  • Video
  • Audio
  • Images
  • Text
02

Interaction model

Guided steps instead of a chat box

The default
Chat. The view on the team was, “Everyone is doing a chat. It looks cool and feels like the tech trend.”
Why I pushed back
A blank chat box expects people to know what to write, what the model can do and what to upload. They knew none of the three, and each wrong guess cost 50 credits.
What I did
Replaced chat with a step-by-step task. Each step asks for one clear thing, shows what is supported and says what happens next. Less to learn, fewer wasted credits, a faster route to a good result.
How we tested it
A Wizard of Oz test with 10 participants let us watch how people really approach the model, and where they got stuck, before anything was built.
03

Trust & transparency

Show the work while the AI works

The problem
Generation takes time and spends credits. A silent wait feels like a gamble, and people can’t tell whether the AI understood them.
What I did
A progress indicator with a live percentage, time remaining and the keywords the model is working from. People can see what it understood, then choose to wait or cancel.
04

AI assistance

Bring chat back where it earns its place

The insight
We never said no to chat. We moved it. The edit step is where people decide whether to spend credits again, so that’s where help matters most.
What I did
An AI assistant in the editor checks whether a request is feasible and helps write a clearer prompt. The next generation lands closer on the first try, with fewer credits spent.

What I’d carry forward

  1. Guide before you generate. When a mistake costs money, the interface should do the explaining.
  2. Show the work. Visible progress is what makes an AI feel trustworthy.
  3. Put help where decisions happen. The same feature can annoy or delight depending on where it sits.
